# CourtMate Product Requirements

**Version:** Hackathon MVP, September 2026
**Launch market:** Bengaluru, beginning with Whitefield
**Audience:** Recreational racket-sport players aged 22-60, primarily 22-40, plus the organizers who keep their games running
**Category:** Game registration, player matching, sport identity, and social activity for racket sports

## 1. Product Thesis

CourtMate helps recreational racket-sport players discover, create, join, coordinate, complete, and share games. It combines nearby discovery, live registration, lineup management, sport-specific skill matching, game history, and social activity in one product.

> **Fill the game -> play with the right people -> keep the circle alive.**

The immediate value is a clear source of truth for every session: who is confirmed, how many spots remain, who is waiting, and what action is needed. The player value continues after the game through completed Rallies, sport-specific CMR, profiles, streaks, connections, and the activity feed.

CourtMate supports six racket sports and keeps CMR, reliability, participation, and social activity attached to the player's profile across every CourtMate game.

## 2. Patchamomma Submission Snapshot

### Idea and use case

CourtMate is a data-driven platform for recreational racket sports. Players discover compatible nearby games, organizers manage registration and lineups, and completed sessions build a persistent sport profile through CMR, reliability, streaks, feedback, posts, and connections.

The implemented experience covers the complete game journey: discover or create, request or join, coordinate in a Rally Circle, manage capacity and waitlists, complete the game, submit feedback, update eligible CMR records, publish activity, and find the next suitable session.

### Data sources

- Player-provided profile, locality, availability, sport preference, and privacy settings.
- Organizer-created session details, capacity, CMR band, format, and visibility.
- App-generated join requests, lineups, waitlists, chat, notifications, feedback, CMR history, follows, comments, and reactions.
- Optional user-uploaded profile photos, game photos, and wearable screenshots.
- Browser location, used with permission for approximate nearby discovery.
- Synthetic multi-sport demo records generated by repository seed scripts; no confidential employer or customer data is used.

### Google technology stack

| Gemini API through Google AI Studio | Parses typed and voice game requests, produces grounded concise answers, summarizes player history, analyzes wearable screenshots, and generates sporty avatar options. |

Google AI is advisory, not authoritative. Gemini may understand, retrieve, summarize, and explain; verified Firestore records and deterministic API rules decide visibility, capacity, membership, lifecycle, and CMR.

## 3. Users And Jobs

- **Organizer:** create a session quickly, share one link, know exactly who is confirmed, handle requests, and recover from dropouts.
- **Regular player:** see upcoming games, join with low friction, back out responsibly, build a history, and reconnect with people they enjoyed playing with.
- **New or returning player:** find a nearby session with a compatible sport level, schedule, and social intensity.

The product addresses four connected failures:

- informal coordination does not provide a durable source of truth for capacity and attendance;
- labels such as beginner, intermediate, and advanced differ between communities;
- cancellations create repetitive organizer work and empty paid court time;
- useful sports connections and game memories are difficult to retain after the session.

## 4. Implemented MVP

### Navigation and Home

The authenticated product has five destinations: **Home**, **Games**, **Ask**, **Leaderboard**, and **Profile**.

Home is a racket-sport activity feed with **Discover**, **Following**, and **My rallies**. It supports completed-session activity, player-authored session posts, correctly fitted image media, likes, comments, post links, deletion by the author, following, and recommended players. Following excludes the signed-in player's own posts. Feed cards prioritize the person, game moment, sport, venue, and CMR movement rather than administrative metadata.

### Ask and game creation

Ask accepts short typed or voice requests to find or create games and to explain the signed-in player's verified CourtMate history. It returns grounded session options and explicit creation proposals; it must not invent players, sessions, scores, or ratings. Responses remain concise and action-oriented.

The structured game form supports sport, area, date, start and end time, singles or doubles, capacity, play style, CMR band, casual or competitive rating mode, and `public`, `followers`, or `private` visibility. Creation validates future time, duration, format, capacity, and CMR bounds. Public creation notifies compatible nearby players.

### Explore and registration

Games uses **Explore**, **Upcoming**, **Pending**, and **Completed** tabs. Explore shows all eligible nearby games on a Google Map when configured, with a privacy-safe visual fallback. Players can filter by text, sport, CMR range, date, and time of day. Selecting a map point or cluster lists games in that area, and an empty result clearly says no games were found.

Every game has a shareable Rally Circle preview. Public and follower-visible games use organizer-reviewed join requests. Private games are hidden from discovery; an authenticated person using the shared link joins directly while capacity remains. Full sessions place additional players on the waitlist. A player cannot submit the same active request twice.

Upcoming is the source of truth for confirmed sessions and organizer requests. Players can add a game to Google Calendar or back out. When a confirmed player leaves, the first waitlisted player is promoted automatically. All mutations update relevant client state immediately and trigger a targeted refresh rather than requiring a page reload.

### Rally Circle and completion

A confirmed game has one Rally Circle containing:

- common chat and lightweight time polls for flexible sessions;
- the confirmed lineup with sport-specific CMR;
- capacity, waitlist, and organizer request decisions;
- game completion and post-game actions.

At the scheduled end time, or when marked done, a game moves into Completed. Games requiring the signed-in player's response appear first with a warning count. Feedback collects a private rating for every other confirmed player and a selectable game-quality score; the selected state remains visible after save.

Casual sessions do not change CMR. Competitive sessions change CMR only after a valid two-sided result is confirmed by every named player. A completed session can be posted to the player's feed with photos and a caption. The published post remains linked to its original game.

### CMR, profile, and leaderboard

CMR is a separate `1.00-10.00` CourtMate Rating for each supported sport. An unplayed sport begins at `1.00` and remains visibly locked until the player builds a record. CMR is a compatibility signal, not a popularity score or an official federation ranking.

The profile presents a player card, full bio, editable profile image and bio, following and follower connections, sport CMR, locked sports, weekly streak, reliability, activity history, and CMR trajectory. Public profiles reuse this presentation with a working follow or requested state. Private profiles expose only the top identity card and a lock notice.

Leaderboard is a primary destination with a compact sport selector and three scopes:

- **Core circle:** connected players;
- **Your locality:** players in the signed-in player's area;
- **Bengaluru:** city-wide sport ranking.

### Notifications and live state

Notifications cover join requests and decisions, follow requests, game reminders, and completion or feedback actions. Opening the panel marks visible notifications read, resolved actions stop appearing actionable, and the bell shows the unread count.

The client combines optimistic mutation updates, explicit cache invalidation, and deduplicated five-second polling while the document is visible. Activity, notifications, connections, profiles, leaderboards, the feed, recommendations, and open Rally Circle data refresh without full-page reloads. Polling pauses while hidden and resumes after focus or visibility changes. Toasts dismiss automatically.

## 5. Product Principles

- **Keep the whole game together:** discovery, registration, coordination, completion, and activity should share one session record.
- **One source of truth:** the CourtMate lineup, capacity, and waitlist replace manual interpretation of chat replies.
- **Low input after play:** scores are optional for casual play; a recreational game still contributes to activity, relationships, streaks, and content.
- **Compatibility before status:** CMR should improve game fit first and support friendly comparison second.
- **Session-linked social:** feed posts, photos, ratings, and comments remain connected to an actual completed game.
- **AI with authority boundaries:** Ask may parse, retrieve, summarize, and suggest; users confirm consequential actions and the API enforces them.
- **Private by construction:** public discovery uses approximate locality and aggregate density, never individual home locations.

## 6. Success Loop And Measures

The target loop is:

> Create or discover -> share or join -> confirm lineup -> coordinate -> play -> complete -> record or post -> reconnect

Primary MVP measures:

- percentage of created sessions that reach capacity;
- median time from creation to full lineup;
- cancellation seats recovered through the waitlist;
- join-link conversion to confirmed player;
- completed sessions with player feedback or a feed post;
- players returning for another game within 14 days;
- repeat games between the same players;
- sessions completed without a manual page refresh or stale-state support issue.
